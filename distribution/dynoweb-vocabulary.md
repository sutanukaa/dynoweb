> **Status:** Canonical copy asset. The words we use **everywhere** — ads, website, App Store
> listing, emails, onboarding, sales scripts. If a line of merchant-facing copy contradicts this
> guide, the copy is wrong. Pairs with [dynoweb-icp.md](dynoweb-icp.md) (who) and
> [competitor-wounds.md](competitor-wounds.md) (what rivals do badly + our counter) — this doc is
> *how we talk to them*.
>
> **Established:** 2026-07-17 · **Method:** three primary-source research passes — real Shopify
> Community threads, r/shopify, App Store listings + 1–3★ reviews, competitor copy. Every USE/AVOID
> call traces to a quote or a listing, not a hunch. Blogspam rejected. Unsourced claims are marked

## 0 · The one rule that governs everything

**Merchants describe every feature in OUTCOME language, never CAPABILITY language.** They don't
want "a heatmap" — they want to know "why is nobody buying." Sell the sale you'll save, not the
tool that saves it.

> The winning pattern across every top competitor listing: **problem → what you'll see → the sale
> you'll save.** MIDA's headline is the template: *"See What's Costing You Sales — Then Fix It."*

**Three hard bans, verified — these words are vendor-speak merchants never use:**

| Banned word | Say instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **"CRO"** | "why visitors don't buy", "lost sales" | Every CRO-titled Shopify Community thread was posted by *vendors talking to each other*. Merchants never say it. |
| **"leaking funnel"** | "traffic but no sales", "where you're losing sales" | **Zero** merchant posts found using it. Only vendor marketing. |
| **"nudge"** | **"popup"**, "exit offer", "cart saver" | No top popup app leads with "nudge." Merchants search "popup." (SmartNudge stays the *product* name; ads/listing say popup — see §4.) |
| **"attribution"** | "what's making you money", "what's actually working" | Zero merchant-initiated use found. It's an ads-industry term. (see §9) |

## 1 · The golden words — the master list

These are verified merchant words. Reach for these first, always.

**Nouns / phrases (USE):**
`lost sales` · `traffic but no sales` · `abandoned cart` · `popup` · `exit popup` ·
`show up on Google` / `get found` · `where my sales come from` · `what's making me money` ·
`drop off` · `stuck` · `friction` · `conversion rate` · `add to cart but don't buy`

**Verbs (USE) — the four merchants actually use:**
> **see · watch · spot · find**

Merchants want to *see* the problem. Every feature headline should let them picture themselves
seeing/watching/spotting/finding something.

**The money words (USE):** `makes money` · `earned you $X` · `save the sale` · `worth it` ·
`stop wasting [ad spend]`. Per repo convention (CLAUDE.md): in copy say **"sales"** and **"makes
money"** — reserve "revenue" for forecast/leak language only.

**Global AVOID list (analyst/agency/vendor-speak):**
`CRO` · `leaking funnel` · `nudge` · `attribution` · `optimization platform` · `AI CRO Expert` ·
`incrementality` · `qualitative analytics` · `engagement zones` · `micro-conversion` ·
`behavioral intervention` · `SERP / meta / schema` (as sell-words) · `autopilot` · `set and forget`

## 2 · Who we're talking to — the emotional state (this sets ad TONE)

The merchant looking for this is **not** an optimizer having fun. They are **frustrated,
financially anxious, self-doubting, and close to quitting.** Verbatim, from real threads:

> *"It was so frustrating that I nearly wanted to quit… it can really shake your confidence."*
> *"Honestly I'm so fed up and put myself in so much debt."*
> *"What am I doing wrong?"*
> *"I could not figure out where that came from. Now I know."*

**Four tone rules that follow directly:**

1. **Lead with the feeling, not the feature.** "Traffic but no sales — and you don't know why"
   mirrors their exact internal monologue. Open there, not with "AI-powered analytics."
2. **Reduce self-blame, restore agency.** They ask "what am *I* doing wrong." Copy that says
   *"it's not you — it's one fixable thing, and here's exactly which one"* converts anxiety into a
   next step.
3. **Money is the emotional core, not just positioning.** They're in debt, they wasted ad spend.
   "Save the sale / show the money" speaks to real fear. Never bury the dollar tie.
4. **Do not add to the overwhelm.** They're already buried in conflicting advice. Promise **one
   clear thing to fix first** — never "a dashboard of 40 metrics." (This is philosophy.md P1 +
   P6 in copy form.)

## 2.5 · Trust & billing — the category's open wound, so make it a pillar

From [competitor-wounds.md](competitor-wounds.md): **billing-trust is the #1 killer of every paid
competitor — not features.** Lucky Orange hit a 7-year customer with a *"2,400% hike without
explicit consent"*; OptiMonk pocketed a surprise *$800 annual*. MIDA's calm billing is *why* it's
the loved one. This is the **highest-leverage promise in the category**, so it earns its own
messaging pillar — say it loud, everywhere.

**USE (trust language — verified against rival scar tissue):**
- `honest billing` · `no surprise charges, ever` · `bots don't burn your quota` ·
  `a real human answers` · `your data stays yours` · `won't slow your store — we'll prove it` ·
  `keep your history` (Clarity forgets after 30 days; we don't)

**The other verified wound = the act-gap.** A real merchant on Lucky Orange:
*"I like watching customers, but where is the value beyond that?"* Merchants can *watch* (free tools
do it fine) but are stranded at "now what?" **So "we fix it, not just show it" is a core line**, not
a feature bullet.

**AVOID (their hate-words — quietly exorcise these feelings from our copy):**
`money hungry` · `took thousands of dollars` · `use at your own risk` · `misleading` ·
`too many bugs` · `infuriating` · `if I can't use it, it's useless` · `slowed my whole store`.

## 4 · Per-feature vocabulary + ready-to-use copy

Each block: **USE** (merchant words) · **AVOID** · **Ready copy** (paste-able) · competitor proof.

### Heatmaps (click / scroll / frustration maps)
- **USE:** "see where visitors click", "how far they scroll", "which buttons get ignored",
  "clicking something that isn't a link", "tapping a product image that doesn't zoom".
- **AVOID:** bare "rage click" / "dead click" without a plain-English translation; "attention
  mapping", "engagement zones", "viewport analysis".
- **Ready copy:** *"See exactly where shoppers click, scroll, and give up — on a picture of your
  own store."* / *"Is your Add to Cart button getting clicks, or getting ignored?"*
- **Rule:** Clarity *introduced* "rage click" and has to define it. If you use it, define it in
  the same breath: *"rage clicks — when a frustrated shopper taps the same thing over and over."*

### Session Replay / recordings
- **USE:** the verb is **"watch."** "Watch what real shoppers did", "see where they drop off",
  "replay real shopper journeys".
- **AVOID:** "session reconstruction", "user journey analytics", "DOM playback".
- **Ready copy:** *"Watch a real shopper hit your store, hesitate, and leave — then see the exact
  moment you lost them."*
- **Proof:** Propel — *"recover lost sales with session recording"*; MIDA — *"Watch sessions &
  checkout to spot where users drop off."*

### Popups / behavioral interventions (SmartNudge)
- **USE:** **"popup"** (the universal word), "exit popup", "exit-intent", "cart recovery",
  "free shipping bar", "announcement bar", "catch them before they leave", "save the sale".
- **AVOID:** **"nudge"** (vendor-only), "behavioral intervention", "micro-conversion trigger",
  "on-site engagement layer".
- **Ready copy:** *"Catch shoppers the moment they're about to leave — with the right offer, at
  the right second."* / *"A popup that fires when someone rage-clicks your size selector beats one
  that fires on a dumb 5-second timer."*
- **Watch out:** merchants are *cynical* about bad popups (*"fire immediately, cover the whole
  mobile screen, ask for emails before I've seen anything"*). **Promise the smart, well-timed
  version** — that's our honest edge.
- **Proof:** Wisepops — *"Cart Recovery… Designed to convert"*; OptiMonk files exit popups under
  an *"Abandoned cart"* bucket.

### Cart abandonment
- **USE:** **"abandoned cart"** (dominant term, more searched than "cart abandonment"). Verb is
  **"recover"** — "recover abandoned carts", "recover lost sales". Plain speech: "they add to cart
  but don't buy".
- **AVOID:** "cart abandonment" *as a headline* (analyst inversion), "basket abandonment" (UK
  vendor term), "checkout funnel leakage".
- **Distinction to get right:** *abandoned cart* = added, left before checkout; *abandoned
  checkout* = entered checkout, didn't finish. Merchants and Shopify separate these.
- **Ready copy:** *"7 out of 10 shoppers fill a cart and leave. See why — and win them back."*

### AI assistant / chat (DynoAgent) — handle with care
- **THE FINDING: merchants distrust "AI that manages your store." They accept "AI that helps, and
  you stay in control."** The distrust is loud and specific — Shopify's own AI push left merchants
  *"angry and confused"*; a merchant reported AI that *"hallucinates and invents new codes… has
  corrupted the organizational structure."*
- **USE:** "helps you", "does the busywork", **"you approve before anything goes live"**, "shows
  you its edits", "you stay in control", "drafts it, you decide".
- **AVOID (each one reads as the thing they just got burned by):** "AI manages your store",
  "autopilot", "set it and forget it", "the AI runs your store", "fully automated", "AI store
  manager".
- **Ready copy:** *"An AI assistant that drafts the fix and shows you exactly what it'll change —
  nothing goes live until you say yes."*
- **Why this works for us:** it maps to DynoAgent's *real* approve-before-apply flow
  (`AgentAction`). Lead with the control, and the honesty becomes the selling point.

### AI product descriptions / copywriting
- **USE:** "write your product descriptions", "saves you hours", "in your brand voice", "sounds
  like you, not a robot".
- **AVOID:** "content engine", "bulk-spun descriptions", "NLG". ("Bulk" as a feature is fine —
  "write all your descriptions at once" — but always pair with the brand-voice reassurance.)
- **The fear to preempt:** the word is **"generic."** Merchants fear AI copy sounds generic.
  Promise *"in your brand voice, not generic filler — and you edit before it publishes."*

### SEO (get found on Google)
- **USE:** "show up on Google", "get found on Google", "rank on Google", "more organic traffic",
  "appear when people search for what you sell".
- **AVOID as sell-words:** "meta description", "schema / structured data", "title tags", "SERP",
  "technical SEO audit", "alt text". These are the *fixes you perform*, not the *words you sell
  with*. Shopify itself has to define "meta description" every time — the tell that it's not
  merchant vocabulary.
- **Ready copy:** *"Show up on Google when people search for what you sell — we fix the behind-the-
  scenes stuff for you automatically."* (mechanism as reassurance, outcome as the sell)
- **[INFERRED]** that merchants specifically find *schema* confusing — strongly implied by the
  volume of explainer content, but not captured from a verbatim merchant post.

### Revenue / "show the money" (Impact + Conversions)
- **USE:** "what's actually working", "what's making me money", "which button makes you money",
  "is it worth it", "the money DynoWeb made you". Concrete money framing wins.
- **AVOID: "attribution"** (industry term, merchants never say it), "incrementality",
  "multi-touch attribution", "revenue attribution engine". Even bare "revenue" is slightly
  vendor-tilted — prefer "sales" / "makes money".
- **Ready copy:** *"See which button, image, and page actually make you money — and which are dead
  weight."*
- **Note:** DynoWeb's *"this earned you $X"* framing is already exactly right. Keep it. Never
  label the UI or an ad "Attribution."
- **Proof:** MIDA — *"See which elements make money & which waste space"*; OptiMonk — *"Optimize
  for real revenue, not just clicks."*

### Traffic sources / which ads work
- **USE:** "which ads actually work", "where my sales come from", "stop wasting ad spend", "where
  your buyers come from".
- **AVOID:** "channel attribution", "UTM analytics", "traffic-source segmentation", "conversion
  path analysis".
- **Ready copy:** *"Facebook sends 500 visitors and $0. Email sends 50 and $1,200. Know which ads
  actually make sales — and stop paying for the ones that don't."*
- **[INFERRED]** the exact "which ads work" phrasing is synthesized from agency/Shopify sources,
  not a captured verbatim community quote — solid, but worth one community pull before locking a
  headline solely on it.

### The overall AI-diagnosis pitch
- **THE PATTERN THAT WINS:** *"tells you what to fix first"* (diagnosis = welcome, they're
  overwhelmed) **+** *"you approve, then we fix it"* (action = merchant-gated).
- Merchants explicitly *want* to be told where to look — but not to have the AI act unilaterally.
  Competitors who say the AI *tells/analyzes* are trusted; the ones who say the AI *does* draw the
  distrust.
- **Our differentiator** — that we can actually deploy the fix (popups, SEO autofix) — must be
  framed as **"and when you're ready, we deploy it for you,"** never "the AI runs it."
- **AVOID:** "AI decides", "auto-fixes everything", "hands-off optimization", "the AI takes it
  from here".

## 5 · The headline formula

From the ICP + emotional state + outcome rule, the master template:

> **[merchant's feeling / problem in their words] → [what they'll see] → [the sale they'll save].**

Worked examples (paste-able):
- *"Traffic but no sales? See exactly why shoppers leave — and win them back."*
- *"You're not doing it wrong. One fixable thing is costing you sales. Here's which one."*
- *"Watch real shoppers hit your store and leave — then fix the reason in one click."*
- *"Free store checkup: see what's costing you sales this month, in plain English."*

**Wound-backed headlines** (each aimed at a documented rival weakness — see competitor-wounds.md):
- *"Stop watching them leave. Start fixing why."* ← the act-gap ("value beyond just watching?")
- *"Honest billing. No surprise charges. Ever."* ← Lucky Orange / OptiMonk billing rage
- *"A real human answers."* ← Clarity's support black hole
- *"Your data stays yours — not fed to an ad giant."* ← Microsoft-privacy fear
- *"Under 5KB. Won't touch your store speed — and we'll prove it."* ← Hotjar/Lucky Orange bloat
- *"Popups that fire when they're actually leaving — not two seconds after they arrive."* ← OptiMonk timing

The App Store hero line should be one of these, **not** "AI-powered CRO platform for Shopify."

## 7 · How to use this

- **Writing any ad, page, listing, or email?** Draft it, then run it against §6. If it contains a
  NEVER word, rewrite — regardless of how good it sounds.
- **Naming a feature externally?** Check §3 — use the marketing name, not the product name.
- **Setting the tone?** Re-read §2. The reader is anxious and near-quitting, not curious.
- **When this doc is wrong, update it with evidence + date.** Living doc; changes by data, never
  by vibe. Two items are flagged **[INFERRED]** (SEO-schema confusion, the verbatim "which ads
  work" phrasing) — close them with one more community pull before locking headline copy that
  rests solely on them.
